# Zaravan — Brand & Business Plan

**Category:** Premium Iranian saffron, direct-to-consumer + trade
**Origin:** Khorasan, Iran — third-generation family growers
**Markets:** European Union, United Kingdom, United States, Canada
**Currency:** USD (primary), with EUR/GBP/CAD display
**Version:** 1.0

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Zaravan is a third-generation family saffron house from Khorasan bringing single-origin, laboratory-verified saffron directly to households and kitchens in Europe, North America and the UK.

The global saffron market is large but structurally broken for the buyer. Roughly 90% of the world's saffron originates in Khorasan, yet almost none of it reaches the end customer with its origin, harvest date or grade intact. It passes through brokers, is repacked in Spain, Dubai or Italy, and is sold at a 5–10× markup — frequently adulterated with safflower, dyed corn silk or the tasteless white style of the flower. The customer pays a premium and has no way to verify what they received.

Zaravan removes every layer between the field and the kitchen. One family, one region, one harvest, every batch lab-tested to ISO 3632-2 and traceable by batch number. The result is genuinely superior saffron at a price that is competitive rather than inflated, because the margin that normally goes to intermediaries stays with the grower and the customer.

**Positioning statement:** Zaravan is the saffron house for people who want to know exactly what is in the jar — and to taste the difference that knowing makes.

## 2. Brand Foundation

### 2.1 Name

**Zaravan** — from the Persian *zar* (gold) and the suffix *-van* (keeper, bearer, custodian).
*The keeper of gold.*

The name works phonetically in English, German and French, carries no unintended meaning in the target markets, and gives the brand a built-in narrative hook: saffron is red gold, and this family has kept it for three generations.

**Tagline options** (in order of preference):

1. *The keeper of gold.*
2. *Red gold from Khorasan.*
3. *Three generations. One field. One harvest.*

### 2.2 Core Value Proposition

> **Single-origin Khorasan saffron, harvested by the family that grew it and verified by laboratory before it ships — delivered worldwide in under a week.**

Four pillars, in the order a customer cares about them:

| Pillar | What it means | Proof on site |

| **Purity** | All-red stigmas, no style, no dye, no filler | ISO 3632-2 certificate per batch; crocin, picrocrocin and safranal values published |
| **Origin** | One family, one region, named village | Field photography, harvest video, family names |
| **Freshness** | Current harvest only, vacuum-sealed at source | Harvest year and batch number on every package |

### 2.4 Brand Voice

**Classic, quiet, confident.** The product is the argument. The copy's job is to give the reader enough specificity to believe it and then get out of the way.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| State facts with precision — grades, crocin values, harvest dates | Use urgency, countdowns, "limited stock", "act now" |
| Write in calm, complete sentences | Use exclamation marks or ALL CAPS |
| Let numbers and certificates do the persuading | Claim "the best saffron in the world" |
| Speak as a family, in first person plural | Speak in corporate third person |
| Teach — how to bloom threads, how to store, how much to use | Lecture or moralise about competitors by name |
| Use restraint in adjectives; one is enough | Stack adjectives ("finest premium luxury artisanal") |

**Register:** the tone of a good wine merchant or a single-estate olive oil producer — knowledgeable, unhurried, slightly formal, warm underneath.

**Vocabulary to use:** harvest, field, threads, stigma, batch, grade, bloom, aroma, single-origin, hand-picked, Khorasan.
**Vocabulary to avoid:** deal, discount, cheap, exclusive offer, game-changing, unlock, elevate.

**Pricing logic:**

### 3.3 Packaging

- **Retail:** amber glass with airtight tin lid — blocks UV, which degrades crocin. Vacuum-sealed inner pouch. Saffron loses color strength in light and air; the packaging is a functional claim, not a decorative one.
- **Label:** brand mark, grade, net weight, harvest year, batch number, origin village, ISO category. QR code linking to that batch's lab report.
- **Outer:** uncoated cream carton, foil-stamped mark, minimal print. No plastic.
- **Gift box:** lacquered wood, brass measuring spoon, folded card with the family story and two recipes.

| Item | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Carrier** | DHL Express Worldwide (primary), FedEx International Priority (backup) |
| **Transit time** | EU 2–4 business days; UK 2–4; US 3–5; Canada 3–6. **Guaranteed under 7 days or shipping refunded.** |
| **Dispatch** | Within 24 hours of order, Sunday–Thursday |
| **Tracking** | Automatic, with delivery-window notification |
| **Free shipping threshold** | Orders over $75 |
| **Flat rate below threshold** | $12 worldwide |
| **Duties & taxes** | DDP (delivered duty paid) for EU, UK, US, CA — the customer pays nothing on arrival. This is a significant purchase-decision unblocker and must be stated plainly at checkout. |
| **Returns** | 30 days, unopened. Opened packages replaced free if the lab values don't match the certificate. |

**Consider a forward-stocking model in year one:** hold buffer inventory with a 3PL in the Netherlands (EU coverage) and one in the US. It cuts transit to 1–3 days, removes per-order customs friction entirely, and makes the under-a-week promise trivial to keep. Saffron's high value-to-weight ratio makes this cheap to do.

## 5. Regulatory & Operational Risk

This section is not optional and should be resolved before a single line of production code is written.

| Risk | Detail | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **Payment processing** | Most major PSPs (Stripe, PayPal, Shopify Payments) restrict merchants handling Iranian-origin goods, regardless of the legality of the goods themselves. This is the single largest threat to the business. | Resolve *first*. Incorporate the trading entity in the EU, UK or UAE; approach PSPs with full disclosure and food-import documentation; line up a secondary processor before launch. Get written confirmation, not a support-chat answer. |
| **US sanctions** | Foodstuffs and agricultural commodities are broadly permitted under US law (TSRA general licences), but the rules are specific about routing, financing and documentation. | Retain a sanctions/trade attorney in the US before accepting a single US order. Do not rely on general internet guidance — including this document. |
| **Import compliance** | US requires FDA Food Facility Registration and Prior Notice per shipment. EU requires compliance with contaminant limits and labelling regulation (EU) No 1169/2011. | Engage a customs broker in each destination market. Budget for it. |
| **Adulteration accusation** | The category has a bad reputation; any quality complaint escalates fast. | Third-party ISO 3632-2 testing on every batch, publicly published. Retain samples from every lot for 24 months. |
| **Harvest variance** | Yield and grade vary year to year. | Multi-farm sourcing within the family network; state harvest year openly rather than pretending consistency. |
| **Testimonials** | Fabricated reviews are illegal in the US (FTC) and EU (UCPD / Omnibus Directive). | The testimonial copy in §7 is **tone reference only**. Replace with verified customer reviews before launch. See §7.4. |

## 6. Visual Identity

### 6.1 Principles

Minimal, luxurious, serene, distinctive. The saffron thread is the only source of colour that matters; everything else recedes. The layout should feel like a gallery wall — large areas of nothing, one thing at a time, generous breathing room.

### 6.2 Colour Palette

| Role | Name | Hex | Usage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Base | Cream | `#F7F3EA` | Page background. Dominant — roughly 70% of any screen. |
| Ink | Soft Black | `#121110` | Body text, headlines, footer background |
| Primary accent | Sargol Red | `#A72E1F` | Rare. Price emphasis, active states, the brand mark |
| Secondary accent | Antique Gold | `#B8963F` | Hairline rules, dividers, small ornament, hover states |
| Neutral | Warm Grey | `#8C857B` | Secondary text, captions, metadata |
| Surface | Bone | `#FFFCF6` | Cards, elevated panels on cream |

**Discipline:** red and gold combined should never exceed ~5% of the visible surface of a screen. Their scarcity is what makes them read as luxurious. The moment either becomes a background fill, the brand looks like a spice-aisle label.

### 6.3 Typography

| Role | Typeface | Fallback / free alternative | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Display / headings | Canela, Freight Display, or GT Sectra | **Cormorant Garamond** | High-contrast serif. Large sizes, tight tracking, generous line height. |
| Body | Söhne or Neue Haas Grotesk | **Inter** | Neutral grotesque. Never below 16px. Line length capped at 65–70 characters. |
| Detail / metadata | Same as body, uppercase, letter-spaced 0.08em, 12–13px | — | Grade labels, batch numbers, section eyebrows |

**Scale (desktop):** 72 / 56 / 40 / 28 / 20 / 17 / 13 px. Headings at 1.1 line height, body at 1.65.

### 6.4 Spacing & Layout

- 8px base unit. All spacing is a multiple of 8.
- Section padding: **160px** top and bottom on desktop, 96px on tablet, 72px on mobile. Err on the side of too much.
- Max content width 1280px; text blocks capped at 680px.
- 12-column grid, 32px gutters.
- One idea per section. If a section contains two ideas, it is two sections.

### 6.5 Photography Direction

- Natural light only, single source, soft shadow. No studio ring lighting.
- Macro detail of threads against cream linen or unglazed ceramic — the texture of the stigma should be visible.
- Field and hand photography: hands sorting threads, flowers at dawn, the drying room. Documentary, unposed, muted.
- No stock imagery, no floating product on gradient backgrounds, no fake bokeh.
- Consistent warm-neutral grade across every image. Never over-saturate the red — real saffron is deep crimson, not orange.

### 7.4 Legal Note on Testimonials — Read Before Launch

The quotes above are **written as tone and format reference only**. Publishing invented customer testimonials as though they were real is unlawful in every target market: it violates the FTC Act and the 2024 FTC Rule on Consumer Reviews in the US, and the Unfair Commercial Practices Directive as amended by the Omnibus Directive in the EU.

Use them as a brief for the copy you will eventually collect. Fill the section with real reviews before the site goes live. Practical path:

1. Ship 30–50 sample units to food writers, Persian-diaspora food creators, and chefs in the target markets in exchange for honest feedback — disclosed as gifted.
2. Send a review request 10 days after every delivery, when the customer has actually cooked with it.
3. Until you have three real quotes, run section 6 with a single line instead: *"Our first harvest ships this autumn. Reviews will appear here as customers receive it."* Honesty on a launch site is a stronger asset than borrowed credibility.

| Phase | Months | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| **Foundation** | 0–2 | Entity, PSP, import compliance, lab partner, packaging production, photography |
| **Launch** | 3–4 | Homepage + store live, seeding to 50 chefs/creators, first real reviews collected |
| **Demand** | 5–8 | SEO content (grades, storage, recipes, adulteration), Instagram short-form from the farm, Google Shopping on branded + high-intent terms |
| **Trade** | 9–12 | Restaurant and specialty-grocer outreach in DE, NL, UK, US; 50 g / 100 g tier becomes revenue backbone |

**Primary channels, in order of expected efficiency:**

1. **Farm content on Instagram and TikTok.** Harvest, sorting, drying, the family. This category has almost no authentic origin content and it is the cheapest differentiator available.
2. **SEO.** "Is my saffron real", "saffron grades explained", "how much saffron per serving". High intent, low competition from actual growers.
3. **Chef and diaspora seeding.** The Persian, Indian and Spanish diaspora in target markets are the highest-conviction repeat buyers.
4. **Paid search last,** only on terms where the traceability angle can carry the click.

## 10. Open Questions to Resolve

1. Which entity and jurisdiction will trade? This gates everything.
2. Is a confirmed payment processor in place, in writing?
3. Which laboratory issues the ISO 3632-2 certificates, and what is the per-batch cost and turnaround?
4. What is the actual annual volume available from the family's fields? This determines whether trade is a real channel or a distraction.
5. Are the family comfortable appearing on camera and being named? The story is significantly weaker without them.
6. Confirm the harvest year to display at launch.
